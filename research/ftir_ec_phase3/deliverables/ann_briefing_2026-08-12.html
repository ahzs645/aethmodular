This answers your July question — why is the intercept negative when nothing at Addis is. Set predicted EC to zero and the intercept becomes a quantity you can go looking for: 18.8–26.1 Mm⁻¹, median 21.5, about 46% of the median Addis Fabs of 47.11. Slopes span 3.4× across those six setups and intercepts 4.3×; C spans 1.4×. And C is exactly MAC-invariant by construction, so the target is well posed whether or not the MAC fork is ever settled.

The caveat, before you raise it. C is invariant to any multiplicative rescaling of the predicted-EC axis — changing MAC is exactly that, and changing training cohort largely is too. So these are not six independent routes converging on 21 Mm⁻¹. The honest claim is "the additive offset is robust to every scaling choice we have made", not "six methods agree".

New overnight, and it is the sharpest cheap test we had. AIRSpec touches only the spectra and never Fabs, yet it halves the intercept, −3.2215 → −1.6151 — which looks like a refutation of "the offset is in x". It is not, because it also halves the slope, and under y = a(x − c) the intercept is −a·c. A y-side gain change must move the intercept in proportion to the slope and leave c alone. It does: slope ratio 0.5406 against intercept ratio 0.5013, and c moves −7.3% where a and b each move ~50%.

04 · Result — is it a generic HIPS zero?preliminary — agent-reproduced, commit pending

No. IMPROVE HIPS runs through the origin — which makes the offset Addis-specific, and leaves two explanations that are not an absorber.

ftir_16 · improve_implied_mac_curveThe same 151,843-filter HIPS↔TOR join the new fits run through; the reproduction gate matched ftir_16's committed values

Attack-plan Tier 1, item 1, answered: regress IMPROVE Fabs on TOR EC and read the intercept in Mm⁻¹. Pooled OLS gives +1.345 [+1.195, +1.485]; trim the top 5% of EC and it falls to +0.200; in the Addis-like OC/EC ≤ 2.27 subset, trimmed, +0.097 [−0.034, +0.218] — the site-cluster interval, which includes zero; the tighter [+0.069, +0.124] is i.i.d.-row and ~4× too optimistic here. At EC ≤ 0 (n = 4,247) the median Fabs is +0.12 Mm⁻¹ with 23.8% of filters reading negative — zero at zero, with symmetric noise. Per site, 170 of 176 are "significantly" nonzero by OLS, but that is n-power, not magnitude: no site reaches 5 Mm⁻¹ (max 4.64). And the arithmetic killer — only 186 of 160,023 IMPROVE filters (0.12%) carry 21.5 Mm⁻¹ of total absorption, so a generic 21.5 Mm⁻¹ zero-offset is not a thing that could exist. Upper bound ~1.3 Mm⁻¹, i.e. 6% of C; the defensible number is 0.1–0.25, ≤ 1%.

The counterweight, and it is real. Two explanations survive this, and neither is an absorber. (i) Curvature. Fabs is concave in EC — Fabs = 7.50·EC^0.796, R² 0.713, passing through zero — and fitting a straight line to a concave relation manufactures a negative intercept with no offset present. Per-site intercepts run at a median 35% of site mean Fabs (IQR 27–50%); Addis is 21.5/47.11 = 46%, and 32% of IMPROVE sites show a fraction at or above Addis's — in data whose true offset at zero is ~0. (ii) A loading-dependent HIPS artifact. IMPROVE's median Fabs is 1.26 Mm⁻¹ against Addis's 47.11, a 37× gap: this rules out an additive, loading-independent offset but says nothing about one that grows with loading. Per-site intercept does grow with loading (r = 0.689), and extrapolating to Fabs = 47.11 gives 16.2 Mm⁻¹ — 75% of C — six times beyond IMPROVE's largest site median of 8.1, so this analysis cannot close it either way.

And separating those three needs a reference we do not have. Both candidate EC references in the committed dataset turn out to be circular, one on each axis. ChemSpec_BC is Fabs/10 rounded to 2 dp — R² 0.9982, implied MAC 10.0003 — so it is circular with x. ChemSpec_EC was written up in ftir_25 as the independent one; it is not. Against EC_ftir at ETAD: n = 175, r² = 0.999693, ratio median 1.0000 (IQR 0.999–1.001), median |difference| 0.0030 µg/m³ — exactly the 2-dp rounding half-width. It is FTIR-EC itself, rounded, arriving through SPARTAN's speciation table, and docs/open-items.md has flagged the same thing in the four-sites data for weeks. The tell was already in the numbers we quoted — its R² of 0.79 against Fabs matches FTIR-EC's own 0.76 because it is FTIR-EC — and it was read as corroboration. ftir_25 has been corrected and pushed (00ab987).

What that costs us, and what it buys. "Go find ~21 Mm⁻¹ of brown carbon" is materially weaker as a framing than it was yesterday morning — you should hear that today rather than after Satoshi does. But the argument that replaces it is cleaner: the offset is not a generic HIPS zero, it is not on the FTIR axis, the MA350 cannot see whether it is BrC, and nothing in hand can discriminate the three survivors because every candidate reference is circular with one axis or the other. That is not a gap in the story — it is the case for the quartz-TOR campaign, made from four directions.

This was the single most decisive analysis available without new sampling, and it is closed. Attributing the 625-nm excess over an AAE_BC ≈ 1 anchor at 880 gives a negative brown-carbon absorption on most days, and closing the ~21 Mm⁻¹ gap would need AAE_BC = 0.316, which no combustion aerosol has. 07 · The question only you can answer

What wavelength is HIPS — 405 or 633? The repo says both.

docs/filter-optics-reference.md marks the SPARTAN photometer wavelength OPEN: "not stated in the EBC SOP, the public CSV header, or the HIPS Drive file… most likely 633 nm to match the IMPROVE photometer, but unconfirmed — do not quote without checking with SPARTAN." Meanwhile RESEARCH_PROGRESS.md:112 reasons from "HIPS 405 nm", and all of phase 3's prose has assumed ~633. One of those is wrong and I have no way to settle it from here.

Why it decides more than a label. At 405 nm brown carbon and dust absorb several-fold more than at 633. A ~46% non-EC share of Fabs goes from surprising to fairly ordinary — and every piece of red-channel reasoning, including yesterday's MA350 route and the dust null from the AERONET work, is aimed at the wrong wavelength. If it is 405, the framing of slide 03 changes before Satoshi sees it, not after.

The second question is narrower than it was this morning. I verified the field directly: at ETAD, HIPS_Uncertainty and HIPS_MDL exist as their own Parameter rows — not as the Uncertainty column on HIPS_Fabs rows, which is 0/190 and is why AGENTS.md said "unpopulated". HIPS_Uncertainty is 190/190 populated, median 2.9075 Mm⁻¹ = 6.17% of median Fabs. That gives σ_x = 0.308 µg/m³ against σ_y ≈ 0.531, so the Deming λ* ≈ 2.96, not 1.0 — and λ = 1 overstates the errors-in-variables intercept correction on the AIRSpec setup by ~55% (−2.66 against −2.09). AGENTS.md is corrected and pushed (d1e4bce), and docs/open-items.md's "HIPS has no uncertainties" item is now simply wrong.

So what I need is semantics, not existence: what does SPARTAN mean by that field — counting statistics, a repeatability estimate, or a propagated calibration uncertainty? Only the last two make λ* ≈ 2.96 defensible as a measurement error. If it is counting statistics we are back to a bound rather than a number.

Before building the notebook, here is what the committed residual table already says. For the raw model the per-season mean residual is Dry −1.25 — the worst — Belg −0.47, and Kiremt +0.49, the nearest to zero. That is the opposite way round from "the dry, diesel-leaning aerosol is roughly right and the wet, charcoal-dominated seasons break". For the corrected model the residuals are season-stable: −2.59 Dry, −2.32 Belg, −2.04 Kiremt. So season sensitivity is a raw-model phenomenon, which is itself the interesting result — and it is consistent with the corrected model's error being a constant rather than a domain failure.

The confound to design around. Dry spans only about 55% of the wet seasons' x-range, so a naive per-season slope is mechanically attenuated: a shorter segment of the same line looks like a different line. The notebook has to be a pooled fit with a season × x interaction, not three separate crossplots, or it will find a seasonal effect whether or not one exists.
