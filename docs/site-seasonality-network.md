# Site season calendars for the SPARTAN network (and the two Santiago IMPROVE sites)

Fixed local month bins for every site in `gallery/app/public/data/spartan_map.json`,
plus IMPROVE ANTU1 and LCON1, which share Santiago's calendar. The machine-readable
copy is `gallery/app/src/siteSeasons.network.json`. The four original calendars
(Beijing, Delhi, JPL/Pasadena, Addis Ababa) are copied unchanged from
[site-seasonality.md](site-seasonality.md), which also holds the analysis rules that
apply here. As there, these are **fixed month bins, not observed weather**: a filter
in a "monsoon" or "dry" bin was sampled in those calendar months, not necessarily
under those conditions. Onset dates move from year to year.

**Confidence:** `source` means an agency or peer-reviewed paper defines these exact
bins for this city or metro area. `regional` means a national or regional definition
(met service, or a region-wide paper) applied to the city. `fallback` means generic
hemisphere seasons with no specific source; no site needed it. Counts:
12 source, 30 regional, 0 fallback
(42 sites). Sources marked "blocks automated fetch" were confirmed
through a quoted copy or search excerpt; re-check them in a browser before
formal citation.

**Papers (28 Oct 2026):** every site also has `papers` in the JSON: peer-reviewed
articles that state its months, each with DOI, a `months_match` note and the quoted
sentence. Calendar variants have their own under `variant_papers` (CODC `palmira`,
Taiwan `outlook`, Addis `dry_feb`, Kanpur `dec_winter`, Bujumbura `nkunzimana`). Every
DOI was checked against Crossref (title, first author, year, journal).

**Audit (28 Oct 2026).** A seasonality audit (pinned to a5a80bc) read the seasonal
passage of all 51 papers, and its findings are applied here. Each paper entry now
records three separate qualifications, because a single confidence label cannot
carry them:

| Field | Values | Question |
|---|---|---|
| `match` | `exact`, `rounded`, `partial`, `inferred`, `unverified` | Does the paper give these months? (`rounded`: day or mid-month boundaries rounded to whole months) |
| `scope` | `local`, `regional`, `national`, `transferred` | Is it about this place? (`transferred`: a study of another city applied here) |
| `checked` | `full text`, `indexed passage`, `abstract`, `not read` | What of it was read? |

Scope is per site, not per paper: Shlipak et al. (2026) is `local` for Addis and
`transferred` for Bishoftu. Patlakas et al. 2019 (AEAZ) and González et al. 2026 (ARCB)
are now verified in the publisher's full text. The gallery's Filters panel shows the
papers as numbered references, "(1)", next to each site's season heading, followed by the
agency `sources`, which are no longer hidden when papers exist. Hover for the paper and
its three qualifications (in amber when anything is short of exact, local-or-regional
and read), click for the DOI; the "(i)" after them shows the calendar's `note`.

Weak spots that remain: Jaipur and Kanpur's national bins cite a Delhi paper
(`transferred`); Talca cites Santiago and Chillán studies; Bishoftu borrows Addis
evidence; Haifa and Rehovot round Alpert et al.'s day boundaries; Bujumbura's
January-only short dry season rounds a mid-January to mid-February window; the
Palmira variant's 1966 paper is `not read`, and its stronger 2022 municipal hydrology
study was seen only as an indexed passage; Addis `dry_feb` rests on a public-health
paper (exact) and an AOD paper that implies, but does not state, a dry Oct–Feb.
**CODC's station identity is unresolved** (named Palmira, placed at Bogotá); confirm it
against the SPARTAN Site Details Sheet before using either calendar in a result.

Still unchecked after the audit: the 1966 Palmira climate passage, the full Palmira
2022 report, direct full-text access to the 14 papers read as indexed passages, and an
official Central Weather Administration source for Taiwan's shifted quarters.
