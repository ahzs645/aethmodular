**Your questions**

- **Look-alikes vs Addis (Figures 4b and 5b):** much of the "different direction" comes from comparing two different
  calibrations. With the same baseline-corrected calibration, both read low: Addis 0.59x - 1.21, look-alikes 0.46x + 0.11.
  With the same raw (SPARTAN-like) calibration, Addis is 2.36x - 6.30 and the look-alikes are 0.70x + 0.01.
  - In µg per filter the look-alikes carry much less EC (median 8 µg vs 34 µg for Addis), so none of them reaches the
    Addis range.
  - Side by side, the baseline-corrected spectra show that, per µg of EC, Addis carries about a tenth of the organic
    absorbance (O-H, C-H, carbonyl) of its look-alikes. They match the shape of the Addis spectra but not the amount
    of organic material per unit EC. Since an IMPROVE-trained calibration reads EC partly through the organic bands,
    it reads Addis low.
- **Kiremt vs Dry:** per µg of EC, the Addis organic signal relative to each season's look-alikes is lowest in Dry
  (0.07-0.12), higher in Belg (0.13-0.22) and highest in Kiremt (0.18-0.28). Kiremt's look-alikes are also the heaviest.
  So Kiremt is closest to what an IMPROVE calibration expects, and Dry is the most EC-dominated. That fits Dry being
  mostly diesel, and it doesn't point to wood or charcoal smoke as the problem.
- **Seasons separately vs together:** within each season the slope is 0.31-0.50, but the season means rise more
  steeply (0.87 from Dry to Kiremt). The pooled fit mixes the two, so it is steeper (0.59) with a more negative
  intercept. Putting the three season-specific calibrations together gives 1.51x - 4.59 (R² 0.55), because each season
  lands at a different level.
